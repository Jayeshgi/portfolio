## Make each project convincing

The repository review supplies the technical starting point; you do not need to rewrite verified descriptions. For each featured project, fill gaps in the following areas where you have useful context:

- [ ] Who the project helps and what problem it addresses.
- [ ] What you built personally, especially for team projects; distinguish your role from the team's work.
- [ ] Two or three features worth demonstrating.
- [ ] Main technical decisions and why you made them.
- [ ] One difficult bug, constraint, or design tradeoff and how you handled it.
- [ ] Outcomes supported by evidence: a working feature, user feedback, a test result, a measured improvement, or what you learned. Numbers are optional; only include numbers you can explain and support.
- [ ] Repository, live demo, short video, screenshots, or other shareable evidence. Identify private, unavailable, or broken links.
- [ ] Dates and status: complete, in progress, archived, or planned.
- [ ] What you would improve next.

For AI/ML projects, also share the task, dataset source and permission to use it, your model or approach, and how you evaluated it. If you report performance, include the metric, evaluation split, and baseline where available. Explain known limitations; a clear experiment and honest result can be a useful project even without a deployed app.

Include your other projects in a shorter archive. You do not need to present every project as a full case study.

## Tell the four-year story

Use actual dates and experiences rather than treating these prompts as assumed achievements.

| Stage | Useful prompts | Possible evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Year 1 | How did you begin? What was your first language or program? What confused you? What finally made sense? | First repository, small exercise, notes, early screenshot |
| Year 2 | What did you start building? How did your tools or interests change? What did you learn from a failed approach? | Project history, course work you can share, team contribution |
| Year 3 | Where did you go deeper? Did collaboration, an internship, a hackathon, or open source change how you work? | Pull request, demo, event result, approved work sample |
| Year 4 | What are you focusing on now? What does your strongest work show? What do you want to do next? | Capstone, deployed project, current learning log, next-step plan |

It is fine if your timeline follows a different pattern. Small turning points, persistence through setbacks, and changes of direction can be stronger than a list of awards. Share only personal details you want made public.
